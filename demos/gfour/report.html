Summary

gFour has what most marketing companies can only claim: 17 years in the industry, founders who ran home improvement businesses, a named 28-person team, and case studies with real numbers. Window Depot of Northeast Ohio closes 70–80% of referral appointments. Centurion Exteriors reports more than $1 million in repeat and referral business. Desert King Windows added 290 reviews in 10 months.

The website doesn't put that proof to work. The best evidence is buried in tabs, PDFs and a reviews page with no reviews on it. The business details conflict from page to page, and the technical basics that search engines and AI assistants rely on are missing or inconsistent.

The five changes that matter most, in order:

  1. Fix the reviews gap. A company that sells 5-star reviews has none showing on its own Reviews page, and a G2 profile with zero reviews. Prospects check this.
  2. Use one address everywhere. The footer says Miami; the contact page and outside listings say Coral Gables.
  3. Correct and source every number. One headline stat has a typo that makes it unreadable, and several claims have no source.
  4. Turn PDF case studies into pages, and lead with them.
  5. Add the technical basics: descriptive titles, image alt text, a share image and structured data.

What's already strong

Real, specific client results. A clear niche. A guarantee. Recent posts on reviews and AI search (AEO). A crawlable site with a sitemap and an open robots.txt.

What's holding it back

Missing social proof, conflicting business details, unsourced or broken numbers, PDFs instead of pages, and a navigation with seven items and five different calls to action.

What we built

A six-page website concept using only your existing content and facts, plus this report. Every number in the concept comes from your current site.

Trust and reputation

High

The Reviews page has no reviews

gfourmarketing.com/reviews/ shows the line “Trusted by 200+ Contractors. Proven Results. 99% Client Retention” and three case studies, but no reviews from clients. gFour's G2 profile shows 0 reviews and says there aren't enough reviews for buying insight. A contractor evaluating a review-generation company will notice this before anything else.

Fix: Run the same playbook you run for clients on your own client base: ask your happiest clients for Google reviews first, then for G2 or an industry directory. Show real reviews on the site (embedded or quoted with name and company), and merge Reviews into Customer Stories so there is one page of proof. Don't add review star markup for your own reviews: Google doesn't show stars for reviews a business publishes about itself.

Medium

Your strongest proof is hidden

Seven testimonials sit inside homepage tabs. The best case study numbers (983 jobs, 24.6x ROI, reviews from 307 to 563) live only on an inner page. Client logos have no alt text, so search engines and screen readers can't tell who they are.

Fix: Lead the homepage with one feature story and its numbers, give every logo its company name, and make each testimonial link to its full story.

Business details and local search

High

Two addresses

The site footer lists 218 North West 24th Street, Suite 324, Miami, FL 33127. The contact page lists 2100 Ponce de Leon, Suite 1090, Coral Gables, FL 33134. G2 and the RoofersCoffeeShop directory both list Coral Gables. Search engines and AI assistants cross-check these details, and conflicts weaken confidence in which one is right.

Fix: Pick the current address and use it on every page, in Google Business Profile, on LinkedIn, Facebook, G2 and industry directories. The concept uses the Coral Gables address because three sources agree; please confirm which is current.

Low

Small signs of an unmaintained site

The footer reads © 2024. The contact page lists no email address. The RoofersCoffeeShop listing mentions an Instagram account that the site doesn't link to.

Fix: Make the copyright year automatic, add a general inbox, and link every active social profile from the footer.

Technical SEO

Medium

Titles, share images and alt text

The homepage title is “Home - gFour Marketing”, which spends the most valuable words on “Home”. The homepage has no og:image, so links shared on LinkedIn, Facebook or in texts show no image. Our crawl found 48 images on the homepage with empty alt text.

Fix: Titles that say what you do and for whom (for example, “Relationship marketing for contractors | gFour Marketing”), a branded share image on every page, and alt text on every meaningful image, client logos included.

Medium

Structured data

We didn't detect JSON-LD structured data on the homepage or case study pages. Yoast is installed (it manages robots.txt), and it normally outputs basic structured data, so this may be switched off or stripped by another plugin.

Fix: Organization data with one address, phone, logo, founding year and links to every social profile; Article data with author and date on posts and case studies; breadcrumbs. Verify with Google's Rich Results Test.

Low

Heading structure and page weight

The homepage uses H2 tags for full sentences of body copy and jumps to H4 and H5 for small labels. The site runs on Elementor with Facebook Pixel and Site Kit, which tends to be heavy on phones. We couldn't run Google PageSpeed from our environment for this review; it's the first thing we'd measure with access.

Fix: One H1 per page and headings that outline the page. Measure Core Web Vitals in Search Console and PageSpeed Insights, then trim unused scripts.

Content and AI search

Contractors increasingly ask ChatGPT, Google's AI answers and Perplexity questions like “how do I get more referrals for my roofing company?” Those tools favor pages with specific, sourced facts, clear authorship and consistent business details. Your own September post on reviews and AEO makes the same point. The site already has the raw material.

Medium

Case studies in PDFs

Centurion Exteriors, Infinity and the “50% of your business” story are PDFs. PDFs are harder for search engines to rank and for AI tools to cite, and on phones they're slow to open.

Fix: Rebuild each as a page with the same structure as the Window Depot story: the problem, what changed, the numbers, a quote, the trade and a date.

High

Numbers that need fixing or a source

  • Who We Help shows “+$1,000,000,0000 Combined Revenue Serviced”. The extra zero makes it unclear whether you mean $1 billion or $10 billion.
  • “Hundreds” of companies (About) and “200+ contractors” (Reviews) can both be true; use one figure everywhere.
  • “87% of home improvement businesses saw a significant surge” and “350% increase in reviews when a gift is sent” have no source.

Fix: Correct the typo, pick one client count, and attach a source to every statistic (“gFour client data, 2025” is a valid source). Your 2026 Home Improvement Industry Report and its 15.6x median ROI deserve their own page: original data is what AI tools quote.

Medium

Thin and aging resources

The Resources page lists 3 blog posts and 3 guides. The guides date from 2024, including “Navigating Marketing Success in 2024 & Beyond”. Posts show no author or visible date. The trade list links about half of its 26 trades to their own pages, and lists “Garage Companies” twice.

Fix: Add an author (a named gFour leader) and a date to every post, refresh or retire the 2024 guides, and build out trade pages only where you have a real story or numbers for that trade. Near-identical pages for every trade can be treated as spam under Google's policies.

Conversion

Medium

Five ways to say the same thing

The site uses “Let's Talk”, “Book a Demo”, “Schedule a Call”, “Get Started” and “Book an Appointment”, and they all go to the contact page. The main menu has seven items, and Reviews, Customer Stories and Resources overlap.

Fix: One action, “Book a demo”, everywhere. Four menu items. The concept shows this structure.

Medium

The contact page in the page source

The contact page says “Please fill out the form below” under “Schedule a demo”, but no form fields or scheduler appeared in the page's source. It's most likely loaded by a script, which is common, but it's worth checking that the form loads quickly on phones, that it works if a script is blocked, and that every submission is tracked as a conversion.

Fix: A short form built into the page (name, company, trade, jobs per year, email, phone), what happens next, and the guarantee next to the button.

The website concept

We built a six-page concept so you can see these recommendations instead of imagining them. It uses only content, numbers, names and claims already on gfourmarketing.com. We rewrote some copy for clarity but added no new facts.

  • The hero shows the product. The newsletter, cookie box, referral check, thank-you card and new 5-star review your clients' customers actually receive, each branded “Your Company”.
  • The gFour Formula as a timeline. Appreciation, reviews, referrals and nurture are shown in the order a homeowner experiences them after the final walkthrough.
  • Proof first. A feature story with Window Depot's numbers, testimonials by trade, and a filterable Customer Stories page.
  • Simpler structure. Four menu items and one call to action. Reviews and case studies are combined, pricing lives on the services page, and the team has a real About page.
  • Built for phones, fast, and accessible, with structured data and complete metadata on the production version.

The concept keeps your current logo and its black and gold colors, so your trucks, mailers and packaging still match. In production we'd use full-resolution logo files, team photos and real photos of the cookie packages.

The plan
